When I became a Principal Frontend Engineer, I expected someone to hand me a list of responsibilities. Nobody did. The title exists on a spectrum between "very senior IC" and "technical lead" that means something different at every company.

After a year doing it at iubenda, here's what I've figured out.

## You are not the best programmer in the room

Or if you are, that's a problem. Your job is not to write the most code — it's to make every engineer around you more effective. That shift is harder than it sounds.

The instinct to just fix it yourself is strong. Especially when you can see exactly what's wrong and you know it would take you 20 minutes. But the engineer who figures it out with your guidance learns something. The one who watches you fix it learns nothing.

## Technical vision is a product, not a document

Everyone writes an architecture decision record. Almost nobody reads them a year later. The technical vision that matters is the one encoded in the codebase itself: the patterns your team follows, the abstractions they reach for, the decisions they make without asking.

Your job is to make the right choice the easy choice.

## You own the problems nobody else owns

Every engineering team has problems that fall between responsibilities. Nobody's sprint, nobody's OKR, nobody's job description. The principal engineer's job is to see those problems and make sure they get solved — either by you or by creating space for someone else to solve them.

At iubenda, that has meant: frontend performance monitoring, the design system strategy, the interview process, the onboarding documentation, the relationship with the product design team.

None of these were assigned to me. All of them needed doing.

## The output is other people's output

This is the hardest reframe. As a senior engineer, you were measured on what you built. As a principal, you are measured on what your team builds. Your fingerprints should be increasingly invisible.

When a junior engineer ships something that would have been impossible without your guidance — and they take the credit — that is the job done well.
